Reliability Is a Product Decision Before It Becomes an Engineering Problem

Engineering can improve reliability, but an organisation first has to decide what level of failure it is willing to tolerate.

Reliability is frequently treated as an engineering responsibility.

The engineering team owns uptime. The platform team owns availability. SRE owns reliability.

There is some truth in each statement, but the framing is incomplete.

Reliability is ultimately a product and organisational decision.

There is no absolute reliability

A system that never fails is a theoretical concept. In practice, reliability is a trade-off.

Improving availability can require additional infrastructure, redundancy, operational complexity and engineering effort. At some point the organisation has to decide whether another increment of reliability is worth its cost.

That decision belongs beyond engineering.

Start with failure tolerance

A more useful conversation begins with: what happens if this system is unavailable?

Customers may not be able to complete transactions. Internal users may have to wait. A business process may continue manually.

Those are very different consequences, and they imply very different reliability requirements.

SLOs force a conversation

Service Level Objectives are often introduced as an SRE mechanism. Their more important function is organisational: they make reliability explicit.

A 99.9% availability objective is a business decision expressed numerically. It implicitly accepts a certain amount of unavailability.

Reliability has multiple dimensions

Availability is only one dimension. Depending on the system, we may care about latency, correctness, durability, recovery time, recovery point, capacity, dependency resilience and operational recovery.

A system can be available and still be effectively unusable. It can respond quickly and return the wrong answer. It can have excellent uptime and poor recovery characteristics.

Reliability needs to describe the behaviour users and the business actually depend on.

Reliability is an organisational capability

The strongest reliability cultures do not treat reliability as something a particular team owns. Product decides what matters. Architecture establishes useful boundaries. Engineering builds for failure. Operations maintains the system. Security considers failure modes and exposure. Leadership decides where to invest.

Reliability emerges from all of those decisions.
